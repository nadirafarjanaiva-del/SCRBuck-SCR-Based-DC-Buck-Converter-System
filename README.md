# SCRBuck: SCR-Based DC Buck Converter System

## Overview

**SCRBuck** is a power electronics project designed to demonstrate the conversion of a higher DC voltage into a lower controlled DC voltage using an **Silicon Controlled Rectifier (SCR)-based switching circuit**. DC voltage conversion is an important requirement in many electronic and electrical systems because different devices and loads often require different operating voltage levels.

This project focuses on developing a step-down DC conversion system that reduces the input DC voltage to a lower output voltage suitable for the connected load. The SCR acts as a controlled semiconductor switching device, allowing the electrical power delivered to the circuit to be regulated through appropriate triggering and control mechanisms.

The project provides a practical demonstration of important concepts in **power electronics, semiconductor switching, voltage control, and DC power conversion**.

## Objectives

The main objectives of this project are:

* To design and develop a DC voltage step-down converter.
* To convert a higher DC input voltage into a lower DC output voltage.
* To study the operation of an SCR in power electronic circuits.
* To demonstrate controlled semiconductor switching.
* To understand the basic principles of DC voltage conversion.
* To investigate the relationship between switching control and output voltage.
* To develop practical knowledge of power electronic converter circuits.
* To create a simple prototype suitable for educational and laboratory applications.

## Working Principle

The SCRBuck system operates by controlling the flow of electrical energy from a higher DC voltage source to the output circuit. The **SCR acts as a controlled switching device** and is activated through an appropriate gate triggering signal.

When the SCR receives a triggering signal, it begins conducting and allows current to flow through the converter circuit. By controlling the operation of the switching and associated energy-storage components, the system produces a lower DC voltage at the output.

The output voltage depends on the circuit configuration, input voltage, switching operation, triggering conditions, and connected load. Appropriate filtering components can be used to reduce output fluctuations and provide a more stable DC output.

This project demonstrates the fundamental concept of controlling electrical power using semiconductor devices and converting electrical energy into a voltage level suitable for different applications.

## Future Improvements

The current prototype can be further improved by implementing a more precise gate triggering and control circuit for better output voltage regulation. Additional improvements could include digital control using a microcontroller, real-time voltage and current monitoring, improved filtering circuits, overload protection, short-circuit protection, and efficiency analysis.

The system could also be expanded by integrating sensors and a display module to monitor input voltage, output voltage, current, and power in real time. These improvements would provide a more advanced platform for studying modern power electronic converter systems.
